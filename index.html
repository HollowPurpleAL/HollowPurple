Lab-in-a-Droplet Acoustic
Levitation

Acoustic levitation is a promising alternative platform for conventional in-vial workflows. This miniaturization approach offers reduced risk of contamination, less analyte adsorption in trace analysis, reduced sample volumes and adheres to green chemistry principles. Explore the proposed workflow and physical properties below step-by-step. Every step is a schematic animation from the experiment.

Key points

No Contact

The sample is trapped in mid-air by acoustic radiation pressure, drastically minimizing the risk of contamination, sample adsorption, or unwanted side crystallization. Additionally, no cuvettes or other sample holders are needed for spectroscopy or X-ray diffraction experiments are needed.

Operation Window

Acoustic levitation enables sample handling across nano- to microliter volume ranges. Depending on the transducer frequency used, several microliter can be levitated. Other microvessels such as microreactor chips operate in the pico- to nanoliter range but inherently suffer from direct vessel contact.

Green Chemistry & Sampling

Due to the reduced volume requirements, smaller amounts of sample, reagents, and solvents are needed. Establishing routine acoustic workflows in clinical chemistry significantly reduces waste production. Additionally, smaller sample sizes per analysis result in lower blood and tissue collection volumes, reducing patient discomfort.

Geron Ratzek

PhD researcher · AK Volmer / Department of Chemistry

Our group is developing instrumental solutions for metabolomics and lipidomics. Currently we are investigating the metabolism of vitamin D with a strong focus on the clinical assessment of vitamin D levels.
Additionally, we are very interested in the integration of acoustic levitation in analytical workflows. Future publications will further develop and integrate acoustic levitation as an alternative sample preparation platform.
